Chapter 5. Official charts and platforms (2010–)

Key point. After 2010, a ranking became a calculation that adds usage and sales across several music services at set weights. The dates when the rules and weights changed are the seams of this period. This chapter covers those rule changes instead of a No. 1 list.

From Gaon Chart to Circle Chart

Gaon Chart launched on 23 February 2010. Reports at the launch said it combined sales data from six online music services and mobile carriers' music services, drawn from about 20 million paying users. The album chart used distributors' data. A 2011 Ministry of Culture article explained that each service's value was weighted by price.

On 7 July 2022 Gaon Chart was relaunched as Circle Chart. It announced data partnerships with overseas platforms such as YouTube, TikTok, Spotify, and Apple Music, and weekly sales figures on its album chart.

Policy changes

  • 8 August 2013. The Ministry of Culture announced measures against bulk buying of tracks. It said it would "pursue" download-centered tallies, a limit of one count per ID per song per day, and less emphasis on real-time charts. The dates these took effect were not confirmed.
  • February 2016. An amendment to the Music Industry Promotion Act banning bulk buying passed the National Assembly's Legislation and Judiciary Committee (ministry release, 29 Feb 2016). The promulgation and effective dates are still being checked.

Weights in broadcast charts

  • SBS Inkigayo revived its ranking on 17 March 2013 as the Inkigayo Chart, run with Gaon Chart, adding a social media score.
  • KBS Music Bank weighted digital 65, broadcast 20, viewer preference 10, and album 5 around 2016, according to its producers. From the fourth week of February 2022 the weights became digital 60, broadcast 20, viewers 10, album 5, and social media 5.

Platform charts

  • In July 2020 Melon replaced its hourly real-time chart with 24Hits, a 24-hour rolling chart, and removed up-and-down indicators. In August 2021 it opened TOP100, combining the last hour's and the last 24 hours' usage.
  • The Billboard K-pop Hot 100 began on 25 August 2011. Its 2014 stop, 2017 return, and the end of the Korean edition in April 2022 rest on secondary sources.

Why there is no No. 1 list here

Circle Chart's terms of use could not be found on its public pages, and its site footer prohibits AI training and text and data mining. Until the terms are confirmed with the operator, this chapter does not build a weekly No. 1 list.
