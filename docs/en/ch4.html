Chapter 4. Sales tallies and gaps (1998–2010)

Key point. From August 1998 to September 2008 the official sales figure was a monthly tally compiled by the industry association from label reports. Over those ten years the monthly sales of the No. 1 album ranged from around 1.7 million copies in the highest month (Sep 2000) to around 20,000 in the lowest (Feb 2008). There is no official sales tally from October 2008 to February 2010.

The association's monthly tally

The first table covers August 1998. Its header reads "Provided by the Korea Video and Record Association" and "Tallied 10 September 1998." Later headers name the Record Association, the Recording Industry Association of Korea, and the Music Industry Association of Korea. The surviving copies, however, are later re-postings, and copies of tables from the same year can carry different names (the copy for January 1999 names the Music Industry Association, the copy for February 1999 the Recording Industry Association). The dates of the name changes are therefore not set from these headers.

In the tables consulted, the column headings say "sales" or "quantity sold"; the word "shipments" was not seen. Even so, the figures are not direct counts of retail sales; they collect what labels reported to the association. Notes such as "some reports have not arrived yet; we will correct when they do" are common below the tables. From September 2006 Digital Discs (DD) were included in sales.

Monthly sales of the No. 1 album

The figure shows the monthly sales of each month's No. 1 album. Because the Wikipedia list gives cumulative totals for five months, the figure uses the monthly sales column of the tables.

The highest month is September 2000 (1,705,127 copies) and the lowest February 2008 (20,697). Why No. 1 sales fell over the period cannot be told from these data alone. Whether the same set of labels reported each month is also not known.

Genre of the No. 1 album

Each album on the tables has a genre column. The figure counts the No. 1 album's genre each year, following the label printed at the time. Of the 122 months, ballad accounts for 44 and dance for 39. Another 27 months were left unclassified: the table gave only gayo (Korean pop) or Local, gave two genres together, or had no genre column (Aug 1998).

Checking the secondary list

The No. 1 album was checked against a copy of the table for all 122 months. The Wikipedia list matches in 120. The Wikipedia table has two rows for April 2001; the first is taken as that month (the second holds May's entry).

  • January 2000. The list carries over December 1999's No. 1. The January table has two rows numbered 1; the first is identical, figures included, to December's top row. The February table's cumulative total (1,302,667) equals January's 676,327 plus February's 626,340, so January's No. 1 is taken to be Jo Sung-mo's Classic.
  • June 2000. The list's source points to the pop table, so the pop No. 1 was entered as the Korean No. 1. The No. 1 on the Korean table is Lee Jung-hyun's second album.

A gap in Korean data from July 1999 to December 2000 had been suggested, but Korean tables exist for all 18 months of that period.

Gaps in broadcast charts

Broadcast charts broke off often in the same years. After Gayo Top 10 ended (Feb 1998), KBS Music Bank began in June 1998 and dropped its rankings in late 2001; KBS did not rank songs again until September 2007. SBS Inkigayo dropped its rankings on 9 February 2003. Around 2004–2006 there were almost no regular broadcast charts.

After the association's tally ended with September 2008, there was no official sales tally until Gaon Chart launched in February 2010. Hanteo Chart continued as a private sales tally.
