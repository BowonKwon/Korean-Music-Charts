Chapter 2. The age of awards (1945–1980)

Key point. Before 1981, "rankings" were mostly year-end awards that picked singers and songs once a year. Selection mixed voter panels, viewer postcards, airplay, and surveys. A weekly ranking is confirmed in print once, on Dong-A Broadcasting radio in 1972.

Broadcast song awards (from 1965)

On 9 May 1965 Radio Seoul presented its first Radio Seoul Broadcast Song Awards. The award continued as the Broadcast Song Awards of the JoongAng Ilbo and Tongyang Broadcasting (TBC). After the 1981 merger of broadcasters it is said to have carried on as the KBS Song Awards; that link is confirmed only by secondary sources.

The year in which the selection method was reported is 1971, the seventh edition: an executive committee made the final decision taking into account the votes of a 180-member panel. Winners were confirmed for 1965, 1968, 1971, 1972, and 1974; for 1967 only the nominees.

MBC Top 10 Singers (from 1966)

MBC's "Top 10 Singers" first appears in a December 1966 article on the concert for the station's fifth anniversary. The method varied from year to year.

Year Method as reported
1977 Viewer postcards and each singer's airplay. Postcards named three favorite singers and one song
1978 The ten singers were chosen by viewer postcards; the top singer among them by postcards (80%) and a vote by the festival audience (20%)
1979 Viewer and listener postcards plus a survey sent to 2,000 people

In October 1987 KBS and MBC said they would change how year-end winners were chosen, citing fairness problems from "bulk postcards." That December MBC dropped its Top 10 Singers award in favor of a year-end festival show, and KBS renamed its award Singer of the Year and scaled it down.

KBS also announced its own top 10 singers in December 1979. The top singer was to be chosen on 30 December on its singers' contest show.

A weekly ranking in 1972

On 21 October 1972 the Dong-A Ilbo reported that Dong-A Broadcasting (DBS) had been choosing a weekly top 10, aired Sundays at 6 p.m. It drew on three things: listener requests, airplay across music programs, and record sales, and it considered only songs suitable for broadcast. The paper said it would print the ranking every Saturday, and tables appeared in the evening edition, page 8, on 28 October and 4, 11, and 25 November.

This is a regular ranking confirmed nine years before Gayo Top 10 (1981). When it began and ended, and which songs it listed, are not yet known.

Status of the sources

  • Winners were read from the opening lines of newspaper articles shown in public search results. Where the list was cut off (MBC women singers, 1979), only part is given.
  • MBC's 1966–1976 lists and the TBC winners for 1966, 1967, 1969, 1970, and 1973 were not found.
  • Whether other broadcasters ran regular rankings in 1973–1980 was not confirmed in the sources searched.
