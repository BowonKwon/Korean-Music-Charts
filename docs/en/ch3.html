Chapter 3. Weekly chart shows (1981–1998)

Key point. From 1981 to 1998 the center of weekly television rankings was KBS Gayo Top 10. A voter panel decided the No. 1. Under a rule that removed any song after five straight weeks at the top, no run at No. 1 went beyond five weeks. The No. 1 list can be checked against newspapers of the time only in part.

How Gayo Top 10 was tallied

On 10 February 1981 the Dong-A Ilbo announced that KBS 1TV would launch Gayo Top 10 on Tuesdays at 7:40 p.m. Linking eight regional stations, it would air ten popular songs chosen by "a voter panel of some 1,300 people from all walks of life across the country."

Reports from the time on the method:

Date What was reported
10 Feb 1981 Chosen by a voter panel of about 1,300
17 Nov 1983 Reader's letter: the show says it ranks by record sales and an "electorate" vote, but the electorate is vague
14 Feb 1986 Until then, a panel of about 1,000 viewers each wrote down ten songs, weighted at 70%, and a selection committee of experts counted for 30%. From the 19th the viewer panel is doubled and weighted at 85%
4 Nov 1993 Return postcards to about 3,000 voters picked at random by age group replaced from the 10th by an automated phone survey; voters' ages now taken into account to correct a tilt toward teenagers; with other networks' chart shows dropped in the fall lineup, it was the only one left
17 Oct 1996 The three networks' chart shows used phone responses, votes, and postcards, and their rankings differed

An encyclopedia account describes "a sample of 2,800 plus a group panel of about 200, combined 8:2 with producers' picks." Its source is a single page introducing the 1990 year-end show. The figures differ from those reported in 1981 and 1986. It may describe a method used at some point after the 1986 revision, but the years it applied are not known.

The five-week rule and the shape of the No. 1 record

On Gayo Top 10, a song that stayed at No. 1 for five straight weeks received a Golden Cup and was removed from the tally. The rule is said to have followed Cho Yong-pil's ten straight weeks at No. 1 in 1982 with Mot chatgetda kkoekkori (못찾겠다 꾀꼬리, "I can't find you, oriole"). An encyclopedia footnote dates the five-week threshold to the broadcast of 26 January 1983. That date was not confirmed in sources of the time.

Of the 241 runs at No. 1 that began on or after 26 January 1983, 85 end at exactly five weeks and none goes longer. Among the 23 runs that began earlier there is one of seven weeks and one of ten. Skipped broadcasts are not counted, and the two songs tied at No. 1 on 25 December 1991 are counted separately.

The cutoff at five weeks follows from the tally rule. It does not mean a song's popularity stopped at five weeks.

In its mid-year and year-end reviews for 1993 and 1994, the Dong-A Ilbo listed that year's Golden Cup songs in order. The five songs of 1993 and the three of the first half of 1994 match the encyclopedia list, order included.

Other networks

  • MBC ranked songs on Show Network around 1990 and renamed the show Yeoreobunui Ingi Gayo in November 1990. It ended in the fall 1993 lineup and returned on 21 April 1995 as Ingi Gayo Best 50, last aired on 17 January 1998.
  • SBS ran chart shows from late 1991 (secondary sources).
  • On 30 June 1998 the Dong-A Ilbo wrote that Gayo Top 10 and Ingi Gayo 50 had been "dropped this year." The final broadcast date of Gayo Top 10 (11 February 1998) is confirmed only by secondary sources.

Status of the No. 1 list

The No. 1 list of 889 rows (888 broadcast dates, including 61 weeks with no ranking) uses an encyclopedia list as its frame, checked against Dong-A Ilbo articles. From July 1990 to early 1992 the paper's Friday pop chart column reported the Gayo Top 10 No. 1.

  • After the show moved to Wednesdays on 22 May 1991, several columns disagree when matched to that week's broadcast. Matched to the previous week most agree, but some still do not. Which broadcast a column refers to cannot be settled, so these rows are marked undetermined.
