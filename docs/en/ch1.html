Chapter 1. The age of catalogs: popularity without rankings (1906–1945)

Key point. No sales ranking is found for the gramophone era. Traces of popularity survive in other kinds of records: a single readers' poll in 1935, counts of censored records, and official notices fixing record prices.

The first recordings and sales

Commercial recording of Korean songs took place in 1906. Columbia recorded in Osaka in February, and Victor in Seoul in November and December. Sales began in 1907. The year often given as that of the "first recording," 1907, is the year sales began (Bae and Seok 2015).

A 1933 article in the magazine Byeolgeongon counts six gramophone companies in Seoul, branches and agents included: Victor and Columbia, joined by Polydor, Chieron, Okeh, and Taihei. It reports that Columbia set up audition sites in ten places across Korea to find new singers, offering the winner an exclusive contract and a place at Shochiku in Japan, and that a separate examination to hire one member of its literary department drew about 60 applicants. It mentions a record price of "one won fifty jeon." It gives no sales or release counts.

Censorship and price controls

In June 1933 the police bulletin of the Government-General, Gyeongmu hwibo, ran an article titled "On the regulation of gramophone records." The database holds only its table of contents, so the text was not read. A Keijō Nippō headline of 2 February 1934 reads "Records banned last year: 94." It appears to mean that 94 records were banned in 1933; the breakdown requires reading the article image.

From July 1940 to February 1944 the Government-General's official gazette carried seven notices that set or revised record sale prices under the price control ordinance (one concerns scrap records). The titles were confirmed; the price tables have not yet been read.

The 1935 Samcheolli record-singer poll

The one source from this period found in ranked form is a readers' poll in the magazine Samcheolli. It began in spring 1935 and the results appeared in the October issue. It had two divisions, men and women. Chae Gyu-yeop won the men's division with 1,844 votes, and Wang Su-bok the women's with 1,903.

Later accounts have given the winner as Chae Gyu-yeop or as Wang Su-bok. The original shows that both are right for different divisions.

The original prints a vote total for each division. The men's 5,888 equals the votes of places 1–5 plus the four runners-up. The women's is printed as 4,243, but adding places 1–5 and the five runners-up the same way gives 6,243, a difference of 2,000. The printed grand total of 10,130 differs by one from the sum of the two printed figures (10,131). Which figure is a misprint is left open until the page image is checked.

Status of the sources

  • Samcheolli poll: names, votes, and totals were checked against the text in the National Institute of Korean History database. It was not checked against the page image.
  • The Gyeongmu hwibo text, the breakdown of the 94 banned records, and the gazette price tables: not yet confirmed.
  • Yearly counts of gramophone releases (planned figure F3) are left out of this edition while the terms of use for the catalog data are being checked.
