Korean Music Charts

Who ranked Korean songs, with what, and how — from the first gramophone recordings of 1906 to digital charts

This site traces, on one timeline from 1906, who ranked Korean popular songs and how. No. 1 entries are checked against newspapers of the time or copies of association releases, and each row records its check status. It publishes no lyrics, audio, or album covers, and builds no measure comparing singers' popularity.

The band chart on this page splits the systems that ranked Korean songs from 1906 to 2026 into 14 rows and shows when each ran. Regular weekly or monthly charts are first confirmed in 1972, and the most that ran at some point in one year is 6 (1998, 2020–2022). An empty stretch means nothing was found in the sources searched, not that no ranking existed.

The band chart below puts the running period of each system on one axis. Color shows the kind of ranking, hatching marks spans confirmed only by secondary sources, and the gray background marks years with no regular chart found.

The age of awards (1945–1980)Before 1981, the "rankings" found are mostly broadcast awards that picked singers and songs; those whose editions are numbered were held every year.Chapter 3. Weekly chart shows (1981–1998)From 1981 to 1998 the center of weekly television rankings was KBS *Gayo Top 10*.Chapter 4. Sales tallies and gaps (1998–2010)From August 1998 to September 2008 the official sales figure was a monthly tally compiled by the industry association from label reports.Chapter 5. Official charts and platforms (2010–)After 2010, a ranking became a calculation that adds usage and sales across several music services at set weights.Chapter 6. Naming the generations: how K-pop idol cohorts are divided (1996–)In the newspaper database used here, generation terms such as "first-generation idols" appear from the late 2000s, and at that time more than one way of counting generations was in use.
